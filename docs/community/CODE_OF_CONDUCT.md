# Code of Conduct

Yutha adopts the [Contributor Covenant v2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/) as its Code of Conduct. The full text of v2.1 applies; this document summarizes and adds project-specific notes.

## Summary of the Contributor Covenant

We are committed to making participation in this project a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, color, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment include demonstrating empathy and kindness toward others, being respectful of differing opinions and experiences, giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback, accepting responsibility and apologizing to those affected by mistakes, and focusing on what is best for the overall community.

Examples of unacceptable behavior include the use of sexualized language or imagery, trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, personal or political attacks, public or private harassment, publishing others' private information without explicit permission, and other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in all project spaces — the GitHub organization, the project forum, RFC discussion threads, project chat, and any official events — and also when an individual is officially representing the project in public spaces.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness behavior that violates this Code of Conduct, report it to **conduct@yutha.dev** (or the equivalent contact in [MAINTAINERS.md](./MAINTAINERS.md) until the dedicated address is provisioned).

Reports go to the project lead and a designated co-reviewer. Reports are kept confidential to the maximum extent practical. Reporters who request anonymity get it; reporters who want updates on the resolution process get them.

We do not require reports to be perfectly framed, fully evidenced, or formally written. If you are not sure whether something is a violation, send the report. The reviewers will help figure that out.

## Enforcement

Per Contributor Covenant v2.1, enforcement is graduated:

1. **Correction.** Private feedback to the offender; clarification of expected behavior. No public action.
2. **Warning.** Formal warning with consequences for continued behavior.
3. **Temporary ban** from project spaces.
4. **Permanent ban** from project spaces.

The project lead has authority to apply any of these. The lead's decision is appealable to the broader maintainer group; the maintainer group can override the lead's decision by majority. (This is the one place we deliberately do not use lazy consensus — for conduct decisions, explicit votes are the right shape.)

## What this isn't

- **A speech code.** Strong technical disagreement, even pointed disagreement, is fine and welcome. The line is at personal attack, harassment, and bad-faith conduct. "Your design is bad and here's why" is not a violation. "You are bad" is.
- **A vector for retaliation.** Reporting someone in bad faith — to silence dissent or to harass through the conduct process — is itself a violation. The reviewers will look at both directions.
- **Limited to the maintainers' definition of comfortable.** The Contributor Covenant's behavioral floor is the floor. We do not define it down because someone protests; we do not define it up arbitrarily because someone wants stricter rules.

## Project lead's commitment

The project lead commits to:

- Reading every report personally.
- Responding within 7 days to acknowledge receipt.
- Resolving (with action or with explicit no-action) within 30 days of receipt where possible; communicating extensions when not.
- Making the rough volume of reports public annually (count and category, no identifying details).
